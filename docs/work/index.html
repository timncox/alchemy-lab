One track, three stages

Work began as a module for the Ableton Move. This is the same engine, built for the Alchemy Lab. A track runs three stages in series. The source (SRC) takes one of six machines: Bypass passes the inputs straight through, the other five make sound from a sample or from the live input. The two inserts (FX1 and FX2) each take one of twenty effects, or Bypass.

Every stage has eight parameters, A to H. What each one does depends on the machine in that stage: on the Low-Pass Filter, E is the cutoff; on Iron Room, it's the stereo width. The knobs on the panel are called A–H for that reason. The machine list says what they mean.

Source on Bypass

An effects processor

Stereo in on J1 and J2, two effects in a row, stereo out on J9 and J10. Filters, delays, three reverbs, distortion, a flanger, chorus, phaser and more, in any order.

A gate on J8 still matters here: it fires the Multimode Filter's envelope.

Source on a sample machine

A sampler

Hold B1 to record up to eight seconds of the input. Put One Shot, Polysample, Slicer or Wavescan on the source, then play it with a gate on J8, pitched by J7. The inserts process it.

The Granulator needs no recording: it grains the live input until there is one.

The panel

Each stage has two pages: A–F, and G–H with the machine. B1 steps the stage, B2 flips its page, and holding B3 shows SETUP. Pick a page, then a knob. The names follow the machines chosen above.

B1Tap: next stage, FX1 → FX2 → SRC. It boots on FX1. Its light is the stage's colour. Hold (just over half a second): start recording the input into the sample buffer. B1 blinks red while it records. Hold again to stop; it stops by itself at eight seconds.
B2Tap: flip between the stage's A–F page (bright) and its G–H page (dim). Red when the processor is busy over 80% of the time.
B3Hold: SETUP, for in gain, tempo, level, pan and dry/wet. B1 and B2 do nothing while it's held. B2 + B3 for two seconds opens Settings.

Turning a machine loads its defaults. When you turn a MACHINE knob by hand, A–H jump to that machine's starting values, as on the Move, and their rings show them. Every knob uses pot catch: after a page change it does nothing until you turn it through the position its ring shows, so values never jump.

The twenty-six machines

Six sources and twenty effects (plus Bypass in every stage). Under each one are its eight parameters, A to H, with where the knob starts when you load it. The MACHINE knob lists them in this order, so the position tells you roughly where to find one.

Sampling and the gate

Record. Hold B1 until it blinks: J1 and J2 are now going into an eight-second buffer. Hold it again to stop, or let it fill. The new recording replaces the old one. Recording works whichever stage you're looking at and whatever the source is, so you can record a phrase through Bypass and then choose what plays it.

Play. A gate on J8 plays the source: its rising edge is a note, its falling edge lets it go. J7 sets the pitch in volts per octave, read when the gate rises: 0 V plays the recording at its own pitch, +1 V an octave up. One Shot plays one voice; Polysample and Slicer have eight, and a new note takes the oldest when all eight are busy. Wavescan runs freely and needs no gate.

The gate drives envelopes too. Each gate also starts the Multimode Filter's envelope, in either insert, and releases it when the gate falls. So a Multimode Filter with ENV turned up is an envelope filter on any input, with no sample involved.

The recording lives in memory only. It's gone when the module powers off. The knobs are saved.

Recipes

Starting points. Each one begins from a machine's defaults: load the machines first, then turn.

Plate on anything

  1. FX1: Low-Pass Filter. FX2: Iron Room Reverb.
  2. FX1 page: E (FREQ) down to taste, F (RESO) up a little.
  3. FX2 G–H page: H (MIX) sets how much plate. SETUP's dry/wet blends the whole track.

Envelope filter

  1. FX1: Multimode Filter. Patch a gate or clock into J8.
  2. E (FREQ) low, H (ENV) up: each gate opens the filter.
  3. A (ATK) to D (REL) shape the sweep; G (TYPE) moves it from low-pass to band-pass to high-pass.

Reverse loop

  1. Hold B1, play something for a few seconds, hold B1 again.
  2. SRC: Slicer. B (MODE) towards the top for a reverse loop.
  3. Gate on J8, a sequence on J7. C (STRT) and D (LEN) choose the slice.

Live grains

  1. SRC: Granulator. Nothing recorded needed: it grains the last two seconds of the input.
  2. E (SCAN) moves through the buffer, C (SIZE) and B (DENS) set the texture.
  3. FX2: Voidspace Reverb for a long, smooth tail.

J3–J6 can be pointed at other knobs from the web programmer. Open Settings by holding B2 and B3 for two seconds; B1 steps its pages. The main page has brightness and presets, as on every Alchemy Lab firmware; page 1 is the firmware picker, for loading another instrument from the card.

Everything you set, all three machines and all of their knobs, saves itself to the SD card as /alchemy/state/work.st, five seconds after you stop turning things, and comes back at the next power-up. It uses no preset slot. Presets saved by hand from Settings hold the whole patch too.

Build it from work-alchemy with make libdaisy once, then make, which gives build/work_alchemy.bin. Put it in /alchemy on the card, or flash it like any Alchemy Lab firmware.

Where it comes from

Work is Tim Cox's module for the Ableton Move, written from scratch. Its shape, a source and two inserts per track, and its machine list are inspired by the Elektron Tonverk. It isn't affiliated with Elektron, and its sounds are its own: each machine was built from a description of what it does, not from anyone's code. The Lab version runs the Move engine unchanged apart from its sample rate.

The three reverbs follow three classic designs: Roomtone is a Schroeder room (parallel combs into allpasses), Iron Room a Dattorro plate, and Voidspace a Householder feedback delay network.

The Alchemy Lab and its software kit are by Hermetic Modular.
