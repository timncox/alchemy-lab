Firmware for the Hermetic Modular Alchemy Lab

tetherUSB MIDI in, voltages out

TETHER turns the notes, clock and starts from a USB MIDI keyboard, or from an iPhone, iPad or Mac, into the voltages a modular understands: pitch and gate for a bass and a lead, gates for kick, snare and hats whose height follows velocity, and a clock. With no MIDI clock coming in, J1 and J2 take a clock and a reset from the rack. It was made for PROTECTOR on the M-Vave FM-1 and SMK-37, whose PARTS setting sends every part on its own channel, but any class-compliant MIDI keyboard or app can drive it.

Since 0.5.0 it also works the other way: voltages into the Lab steer PROTECTOR's effects, and the FM-1's sound comes out of the Lab over the same USB cable, with the Lab's J1 and J2 going back into the FM-1. The FM-1 plugs straight in, with no hub.

Plugging in

Everything goes through the Lab's front USB-C, and since v0.2.0 it works both ways round. TETHER finds out by itself which way it's plugged in.

The Lab is the host

A keyboard, straight in

Plug the FM-1, the SMK-37 or any class-compliant USB MIDI keyboard into the Lab. B3 goes green and flashes white with each message.

The Lab puts no power on its USB port (it's built that way; firmware can't change it), and a keyboard's USB usually only wakes up with 5 V on the cable. So most keyboards, the SMK-37 included, need a powered USB hub between them and the Lab. Straight in, B3 goes amber, then red with one blink.

The FM-1 doesn't: with CLOCK OUT on LAB it runs the USB link itself and plugs straight in. See FM-1 straight in.

The Lab is the device

Into an iPhone, iPad or Mac

Plug the Lab into a phone, tablet or computer and it shows up as a MIDI device named TETHER. B3 goes cyan. Pick TETHER as the output in Loadout, a DAW or any MIDI app and its notes drive the jacks, just as a keyboard's would.

With a USB hub on the iPhone, the FM-1 and the Lab can both hang off it: route the FM-1 to TETHER in an app.

When the FM-1 runs the link (LAB), the Lab is its device in the same way, so B3 is cyan then too.

How it decides. The Lab has one port and nothing in the hardware says what's on the other end, so until something answers, the port takes turns: a host for 1.5 seconds, then a device for 2.5. Whatever is plugged in is found within about four seconds, and the port stays that way until it's unplugged. Unplugging lets go of any held notes.

Dim red: nothing plugged in, or still taking turns.
Amber: something answered; setting it up.
Red: it couldn't set up what's plugged in. Count the dark blinks: 1 it never answered (most often no power on the cable: use a powered hub, or LAB on the FM-1), 2 lost while being set up, 3 lost while being configured, 4 no MIDI on it.
Green: a keyboard's MIDI is coming in.
Cyan: an iPhone, iPad, Mac, or the FM-1 on LAB, is playing TETHER.
Blue: Mac mode (below), for the web tools and file transfer.
Mac mode. Hold B1 while powering up and the port stays the Mac tools' device instead (HostLink, the web control page, file transfer). It isn't remembered: the next power-up is back to MIDI. While TETHER is playing MIDI the Mac tools can't reach it.

With PROTECTOR

PROTECTOR 0.4.6 and later has a PARTS setting for CLOCK OUT. It sends the clock, Start and Stop, every part's notes over USB, each on its own channel, and the section (A to D) as CC 85 on channel 16. It's off until you turn it on, and it adds nothing while it's off.

M-Vave FM-1

CLOCK OUT to PARTS

  1. Press SEQ until the screen shows the SONG page (PATTERN, STEPS, then SONG).
  2. Tap SEL for the bottom row: CLOCK IN, CLOCK OUT, LEVEL, LOOP.
  3. Turn the second knob, CLOCK OUT, all the way to PARTS.
  4. Plug the FM-1 into the Lab and press PLAY.
M-Vave SMK-37

CLOCK OUT to PARTS

  1. Press SCALE for the SONG page.
  2. Tap KNOB BANK for the lower row: K5 is CLOCK IN, K6 is CLOCK OUT.
  3. Turn K6 all the way to PARTS.
  4. Plug the SMK-37's USB-C into a powered hub and the hub into the Lab (straight in, its USB gets no power and won't answer). Wait for B3 to go green, then press PLAY.
ChannelPartOn a fresh TETHER

A note can leave PROTECTOR up to 6 ms before you hear it from the keyboard, so the jacks and the keyboard's own sound line up closely. A SLIDE step's next note starts before the last one ends, which TETHER plays as a glide when GLIDE is up and NOTES is LEGATO. Muted parts send nothing new.

FM-1 straight in: CLOCK OUT on LAB

The FM-1 can run the USB link itself, the way a computer would, so it plugs straight into the Lab with no hub. It needs PROTECTOR DIRECT TEST 0.4.8.4, which Loadout installs from its firmware list, and TETHER 0.5.0 for the audio. LAB sends everything PARTS does, plus the audio both ways and the CV in below.

  1. Press SEQ until the screen shows the SONG page, and tap SEL for the bottom row.
  2. Turn the second knob, CLOCK OUT, one step past PARTS to LAB.
  3. Plug the FM-1's USB-C straight into the Lab's front USB-C. No hub.
  4. Watch the top of the FM-1's screen: LAB FOUND · PARTS + AUDIO, then about 1.5 s later LAB AUDIO · OUT ON IN ON. B3 on the Lab goes cyan. Press PLAY.

Popups are brief. Each shows for about a second across the top of whatever page you're on, then the screen goes back to normal. Once it's connected nothing more shows; to see them again, unplug and replug. LAB is never kept: after a power-up CLOCK OUT reads PARTS, and the FM-1 works with the phone and Loadout as usual. While LAB is on, the phone can't reach the FM-1 over USB. The SMK-37 has no LAB yet: use PARTS and a hub.

The panel

Six knobs on five layers. With no button held, they set up the voice that B1 picks (B1 lit amber for voice A, cyan for B, purple for C). Hold B1 for MORE (that voice's envelope, and J1 and J2), B2 for MASTER, B3 for JACKS; tap B1 while holding B3 for the second JACKS page. Pick a layer, then a knob.

B1Tap: the next voice, A, B or C. Its light is that voice's colour. Hold: the MORE layer.
B2Tap: all notes off. Hold: the MASTER layer. Its light flashes white on each beat of the incoming clock, and stays dim amber with no clock.
B3Tap: a 300 ms test note on the selected voice. Hold: the JACKS layer (tap B1 while holding it for page 2). Its light is the USB port (see Plugging in).
B2+B1Calibration (see Tuning).
B2+B3Held for two seconds: Settings, with the firmware picker on its Firmware page.

Knobs catch. After you change voice or layer, a knob does nothing until you turn it to where its ring shows; nothing jumps. Every change saves itself five seconds after you stop turning, and comes back at the next power-up.

Jacks

The ten jacks sit in two rows of five, numbered down each column from the left: odd ones along the top, even ones under them. J3 to J10 each have a job you can change on the JACKS layer or from Loadout; these are the jobs on a fresh module (layout BAND). J1 and J2 are inputs: the Lab's hardware can't make them outputs.

Picking a layout

The lights are the rings around the knobs, not the jacks: on the JACKS layers each knob's ring shows its jack's job in that job's colour.

  1. Hold B3, and keep holding it the whole time: letting go leaves the page.
  2. Tap B1 once for page 2. The top-left knob is J9, the top-right knob J10, and the middle-left knob the layout.
  3. The middle-left ring shows five dim spots, BAND, CLOCK, CV IN, AUDIO and CUSTOM, with one lit. AUDIO is the fourth, at about 2 o'clock. If another is lit, turn that knob all the way left, then up to about 2 o'clock.
  4. With AUDIO, the top-left and top-right rings are pink: J9 and J10 are the audio. Inputs on J3 to J8 show teal. Let go of B3.

CV in

J3 to J8 can each be given an input job: IN FILTER, IN REVERB, IN DELAY, IN ECHO, IN GLITCH, IN PUMP, IN RUMBLE, IN DRIVE, IN COMB, IN PHASER, IN FLANGER, IN CRUSH, IN SCENE or IN MORPH (teal on the ring). The jack then reads a voltage and plays PROTECTOR's effects pad with it, over USB, to the FM-1 on LAB or to a computer. PROTECTOR needs no change. Layouts CV IN and AUDIO put IN FILTER on J5 and IN DELAY on J6.

Audio over USB

From 0.5.0 the Lab is a USB audio interface as well as the TETHER MIDI device, over the same cable. With the FM-1 on LAB:

FM-1 to the Lab

J9 and J10

With J9 and J10 on AUDIO L and AUDIO R (layout AUDIO), the FM-1's sound comes out of them at Eurorack level: ±5 V at full volume, 10 V peak to peak. It's the FM-1's whole stereo mix, after its effects, and the FM-1's volume knob sets how loud it is. Patch them straight into a mixer or a module; no gain stage needed. With nothing streaming they're silent.

The Lab to the FM-1

J1 and J2

Whatever goes into J1 (left) and J2 (right) plays out of the FM-1, mixed in after PROTECTOR's effects. They take Eurorack-level audio; start a hot source a little down, as they clip near ±5 V. J1 and J2 keep their clock and reset jobs too (ignored while MIDI clock comes in).

Picking by hand. Choosing the AUDIO layout sets J9 to AUDIO L and J10 to AUDIO R. To set one jack yourself, its knob on page 2 goes all the way right for AUDIO R, a hair back from the end for AUDIO L; both show pink and look the same on the ring, so pick the layout unless you need something odd, like J9 on AUDIO L with J10 still on PITCH B. There's no mono mix of left and right. What comes in on J1 and J2 doesn't go back out of J9 and J10. A computer or iPhone sees the Lab as a class-compliant audio interface too (44.1 kHz, 16-bit stereo), but that hasn't been tried yet.

Drums, J1 and J2

Drum gates. Each drum's jack goes up when its note starts and down when it ends, at least TRIG LENGTH, so a module whose sound follows the gate's length hears how long each hit was held. Entity Ultra Perc's manual suggests just that. Set a drum's MODE to TRIG for a fixed-length trigger instead.

Velocity. With VEL on (fresh for KICK, SNARE and HATS), a hit at velocity 127 gives 5 V and the softest gives FLOOR, 2.5 V to start, so even a soft hit still clears a trigger input that fires at about 2.5 V. Every jack on the Lab tops out at about 5 V, so none reaches the 8 to 10 V that some drum modules want for their loudest. Metalloid plays its normal level at 5 V and quieter below it, so its hits get softer with velocity but never louder than normal.

Two hits in a row. When a hit starts as the last one ends, or while it's still up, the jack drops for 3 ms first, so the module hears two hits, not one long one. That hit comes out up to 3 ms late.

Timing. J7 and J8 change within a millisecond of the note. J3 to J6 change about once a millisecond, so a drum there (the hats on J5) can come up to 1 ms after the kick and snare. Those four also pause for a few milliseconds while the screen is drawn; no hit or clock pulse is lost in a pause, though one can come a few milliseconds late, and each low and each hit on them lasts at least 3 ms.

J1 and J2. With no MIDI clock coming in, J1 takes a clock (a pulse above about 1 V; 4 a beat to start, set on MORE) and J2 a reset or a start/stop. The first pulse on J1 starts the playing, and three of its pulses' worth of nothing stops it. MIDI clock always wins: while it's coming in, J1 and J2 are ignored.

From Loadout

Plug the Lab into an iPhone or iPad (B3 cyan), open Loadout, and go to PRESETS > TETHER. It reads what each jack does, each drum's mode and softest level, the three voices' channels and envelopes, J1's clock and J2, and lets you change them there. Nothing changes on the Lab until you tap Send; calibration stays as set on the Lab. It needs TETHER 0.3.0 or newer and a Loadout build with the TETHER screen.

Tuning the pitch outputs

Every module's outputs are a little different, so each voice has its own calibration. You need a tuner and the oscillator you'll play.

  1. Patch J9 (or J10) into the oscillator's 1 V/oct input and the oscillator into a tuner. Select that voice with B1.
  2. Hold B2 and tap B1: both pitch jacks send 1 V, with both gates up. Still holding B2, turn the top-left knob, CAL OFFSET, until the tuner reads the note you expect.
  3. Tap B1 again (B2 still held): 3 V, two octaves higher. Turn the top-right knob, CAL SCALE, until it reads exactly two octaves above.
  4. Repeat the two until both land. A third tap of B1 turns the test voltage off; letting go of B2 ends calibration. It saves itself like everything else.

Install, and going back to Belt

TETHER is a second firmware that lives on the card beside Belt. Put the file in the card's alchemy folder, then switch to it from the Firmware page.

Download tether_alchemy.bin (v0.5.0, 256 KB). Its SHA-256 is 6ad60b35f0d3eb94ae7d1cdb8f77b70e64494d76ba00e02a3b0ed4cc9a596de2.

Coming from 0.3 or 0.4: your settings and calibration carry over; pick the AUDIO or CV IN layout to use the new jacks.

Coming from 0.1 or 0.2: TETHER 0.3 keeps its settings in a new form, so it starts from its fresh settings (layout BAND); set the calibration and the screen's brightness again.

Simplest

A card reader

Take the microSD card out of the Lab, put it in a card reader, and copy tether_alchemy.bin into its alchemy folder.

Plugging the Lab itself into the Mac won't do this: a running firmware shows up as a USB device, not as a disk, so the card never appears in Finder.

Card stays in

Over USB, from the Mac

Power the Lab up holding B1 (Mac mode; B3 blue), plug it into the Mac, and from the Belt repo run:

node tools/hostlink-fs.mjs put tether_alchemy.bin /alchemy/tether_alchemy.bin --overwrite

The firmware writes the file to the card for you and checks it. It needs Mac mode: while TETHER is playing MIDI the Mac can't reach it this way.

Without the card

The web programmer

Hold B3 while powering up for update mode and flash the file with Hermetic's web programmer in Chrome or Edge. That replaces the firmware that's running, so Belt then comes back the same way.

Switching to TETHER

  1. Hold B2 and B3 for two seconds: Settings.
  2. Tap B1 to step to the Firmware page.
  3. Turn the top-left knob to pick tether_alchemy.bin.
  4. Turn the top-right knob all the way down, then all the way up. TETHER starts.

Going back to Belt

The same four steps, picking belt_alchemy.bin instead. Settings works the same in every firmware of this family. TETHER and Belt each keep their settings in their own preset slot (TETHER 10, Belt 14), so switching between them loses neither.
