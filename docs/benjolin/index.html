Benjolin is Rob Hordijk's Benjolin: two oscillators, the rungler shift register and a resonant filter, on three pages. JOLIN has the oscillators and the rungler, FILTER the filter and the outs, and PATCH is a small patchbay. It is the Hypjolin firmware with the Hypster taken out.

A Benjolin, and only that

The Benjolin is a "noise box that is bent by design". Two oscillators feed a rungler, an eight-step shift register clocked by one oscillator and fed by the other. Its last three steps make a stepped voltage that goes back into the oscillators' pitch, so the whole thing keeps rearranging itself. The result sweeps a resonant filter. It is chaos in steps: rhythmic, gritty, sometimes almost a melody.

Why this exists. Hypjolin, the bigger firmware this comes from, puts the Benjolin next to a hyperchaos generator and spreads the two over five pages. A tester found it worked well, but that past three pages the module started to feel tedious, and asked for a simpler Benjolin with three pages or fewer. This is that: the Benjolin alone, exactly three pages, and one of them still a patch page so it keeps the feel of plugging cables.

Jolin · Filter

The Benjolin as before

Osc A and Osc B, 18 octaves each, from slow pulses to buzzing tones. The rungler pushes their pitch and the filter's cutoff. LOOP freezes the pattern into a repeating figure. These two pages are Hypjolin's, with the Hypster's options taken out of the selectors.

Outputs: triangle A, triangle B, pulse A, pulse B, XOR, PWM, rungler, lopass.

Patch

The patchbay on one page

The Benjolin has three CV inputs: Osc A, Osc B and the filter. Six knobs is exactly three pairs, so each input gets a row: what on the left, how much on the right, read the way a cable runs. The whole patch is in view at once and no button has to be held.

The panel

Three pages of six knobs. B2 steps through them: Jolin, Filter, Patch, and back to Jolin. Nothing is held for a page. Pick a page, then a knob. The rings here sit where the module puts them on its first boot.

The lights

  • B1: the LOOP switch, after the J6 gate. Off is the rungler's level in blue; on is cyan with the level riding on it.
  • B2: the page's colour (blue, cyan, amber). Red when the processor averages over 80 %.
  • B3: triangle A as red and triangle B as blue; white for a moment on a new pattern.
  • At power-on the P1 ring shows the previous session's worst processor load for 2.5 seconds.

The rungler

Each time Osc B's pulse rises, the register shifts one step and a new bit comes in: Osc A's pulse, XOR the bit falling off the end. The last three bits, marked with a cyan line, are read as a number from 0 to 7. That is the rungler's stepped voltage, and RUN>A, RUN>B and RUN>F send it to the two oscillators and the filter.

With LOOP on, the LOOP knob offsets Osc A's comparator. Near noon new bits still arrive. Towards either end the comparator stops changing and the register just goes round, so the pattern locks into a loop of 8 steps (or 16, when it comes back inverted).

B3 loads a new random pattern into the register. With LOOP on and the LOOP knob at either end, the rungler then holds it, so B3 is "give me another loop". With LOOP off it only nudges a pattern that keeps changing anyway.

Slowed down 40 times here so you can watch it step. On the module the same knobs reach audio rate.

Patching

The PATCH page (amber) is three rows, one per input: → Osc A, → Osc B and → Filter. The left knob of a row picks the source: Off or any of the eight outputs. The right knob sets how much, and which way: noon is nothing, right is positive, left is inverted. Full scale is 5 V at that input, which is five octaves. The jacks J3–J5 add on top, at 1 V per octave.

It boots already patched: Triangle B → Osc A at +0.15 (three quarters of an octave, the Benjolin's classic cross-patch, a wobble at Osc B's rate), the Osc B row parked on Pulse A at noon (so off), and Triangle B → Filter at +0.10.

The wobble, deeper

  1. From first boot: PATCH, the Osc A cv knob (top right).
  2. Right for more of Triangle B in Osc A's pitch; past noon to the left for the same wobble inverted.
  3. Turn Osc B on JOLIN to change how fast it wobbles.

Stepped melody

  1. JOLIN: OSC A at audio, OSC B slow (a few Hz), RUN>A up.
  2. B1 for LOOP, then the LOOP knob to one end.
  3. An 8- or 16-step riff. Tap B3 for a different one; turn the knob back towards noon to let it mutate.

The rungler, inverted

  1. PATCH: → Osc B to Rungler.
  2. Osc B cv left of noon.
  3. Where RUN>B only pushes the clock up, this pulls it down as the steps rise.

Clocked from outside

  1. Settings: Clock = J2, and a clock into J2.
  2. A gate into J6 flips LOOP while it is high.
  3. The rungler steps in time, and a sequencer decides when it repeats.

Saving

Everything you set, all three pages, Settings and the LOOP switch, saves itself five seconds after you stop turning things, and comes back as you left it. It saves to the SD card, as its own file: /alchemy/state/benjolin_alchemy.st. It never saves while a button is held, Settings is open or the firmware picker is writing.

Keep the card in. If the card couldn't be read at power-on, the module starts from the defaults and, for that session, won't save over a file that is already there, so the panel you saved is still waiting next time.

The first boot starts from the defaults above, already moving. The file is Benjolin's alone: Hypjolin's saved panel can't load into Benjolin, or the other way round.

Get it on your module

Not downloadable yet. Benjolin goes on the download page once it has been played on a module; until then the steps below are how it will install.

From the SD card

Format a microSD card of 32 GB or less as FAT32, make a folder called alchemy at the top, and copy benjolin_alchemy.bin into it. On a Mac, run dot_clean on the card before ejecting it.

On any Alchemy Lab firmware with the picker, hold B2 and B3 for two seconds to open Settings, and tap B1 to reach the Firmware page. Turn the top-left knob to choose Benjolin, then turn the top-right knob down a little and all the way up to load it. It checks the file, writes it and restarts as Benjolin.

With the web programmer

Put the module into update mode: hold B3 (the bottom button) while you power it on. The rings breathe slowly and the B3 lights stay on. Connect the front USB-C to a computer with a Chromium browser (Chrome, Edge or Brave) and flash the file with Hermetic's web programmer. The module stays in the rack the whole time.

While Benjolin is running, the web programmer's file panel can also reach the card over USB.

Build it yourself

Its source isn't public yet. When it is, it'll be in hypjolin-alchemy: make libdaisy once, then make FW=benjolin, which gives build-benjolin/benjolin_alchemy.bin. Plain make still builds Hypjolin.

Where it comes from

The Benjolin is Rob Hordijk's design. This firmware comes to it by way of the Hypjolin by Triglav Modular in Budapest: an edition of ten, commissioned by Goodiepal and Bananskolen, released anti-copyright with its circuit, panel files and manual at github.com/triglav-modular/Hypjolin. The Hypjolin pairs the Benjolin with Ian Fritz's Hypster; this build keeps only the Benjolin half.

This firmware models the circuit from that material. It isn't affiliated with Triglav or Rob Hordijk's estate, and it hasn't been compared by ear with the real thing. Some details had to be invented to fit the Lab: the PATCH page as three source-and-amount rows and its starting patch, how the LOOP knob locks the rungler, B3's new pattern, the J6 loop gate, and the filter's exact model. The design notes mark every such choice.
