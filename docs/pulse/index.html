pulsea master clock and eight clocked outputs

Pulse keeps the time for the rest of your rack. It runs a clock, from tap tempo or from a clock you plug into J1, and sends eight outputs from J3 to J10. Each one divides or multiplies the beat, and it can be a gate, a trigger, an LFO, an envelope or a random voltage, with its own euclidean pattern, chance and swing.

The eight outputs as a fresh module starts, two bars at a time. Pick an output and change it; the module does the same from the OUTPUT and PATTERN layers.

One beat, eight ways to read it

Everything counts from one clock. Each output has a ratio to it: x2 runs twice as fast as the beat, /4 once a bar. Then a waveform decides what each cycle looks like: a gate held for part of it, a short trigger, a ramp, a triangle, a sine, a falling envelope, or a new random level.

Because every output counts from the same clock, they always line up again on the downbeat. A reset (from B3 or J2) brings them all back to the top together, and the random and chance outputs replay the same sequence each time, so resetting every few bars turns randomness into a loop.

Clocks

Gates and triggers

Quarters, eighths, a bar gate, a triplet feel from x1.5, odd meters from /5 and /7. Gate width runs up to 100%, which ties notes into one long gate.

Modulation

LFOs in time

Saw, ramp, triangle with a skew, sine, and smooth random, all locked to the tempo. LEVEL and OFFSET place them anywhere in ±5 V; PHASE shifts one against the others.

Rhythm

Patterns

Spread any number of hits over up to 32 steps, rotate the pattern, let chance drop hits, and swing pairs of steps from straight to a heavy shuffle.

The panel

Six knobs on three layers. The OUTPUT layer edits the selected output; hold B3 for its PATTERN layer; hold B2 for the MASTER layer, which is the clock itself. Pick a layer, then a knob.

B1Tap: the next output, 1 → 8 → 1. Hold and turn the top-left knob to jump to any output; its ring shows all eight in their colours. B1's light is the selected output, flashing with it.
B2Tap: tap tempo. Two to four taps set the BPM (a pause of two seconds starts a new count). Hold: the MASTER layer. Its light flashes on the beat, amber on the internal clock and blue when it's following J1.
B3Tap: stop, or start from the top. Hold: the PATTERN layer. Green while running, red when stopped.
B2+B3Held for two seconds: Settings. Brightness, presets and the J7/J8 switch on the first page; B1 steps to the firmware picker; B3 leaves.

Knobs catch. Six knobs serve eight outputs and three layers, so after you change output or layer a knob does nothing until you turn it to the value its ring shows. Until then a dim dot on the ring marks where the knob physically is. Nothing ever jumps.

Patterns, chance and swing

STEPS turns an output into a euclidean pattern: each cycle of the output is one step, and FILLS hits are spread over the steps as evenly as they'll go. Three hits in eight steps is the tresillo; five in eight, the cinquillo; seven in sixteen, a samba. ROTATE moves the pattern's start. Turning STEPS up from off starts it half full, so you hear it at once. A step with no hit stays at 0 V (the random waves hold their level instead).

CHANCE rolls for every hit; at 50% about half get through. SWING delays every second cycle: 50% is straight, 66.7% a triplet shuffle, 75% as far as it goes. SLEW glides the output from one level to the next, up to about a second, which turns stepped random into a wandering line and softens a gate into a swell.

Chance and random draw from a sequence that starts again at every reset. Reset every bar, or every four, and the "random" parts repeat exactly.

Following another clock

Plug a clock into J1 and Pulse follows it. Set how many pulses make a beat with PPQN on the MASTER layer: 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 24 (the default, as MIDI clock and most sequencers' sync outputs use) or 48. Each pulse puts the count back where it should be, so multiplied outputs stay locked and a late pulse makes them wait instead of drifting. When J1 goes quiet for three of its periods, Pulse carries on at its own BPM.

J2 does one of two things, chosen on the MASTER layer: Reset sends everything back to the top, or Start/Stop toggles the clock with each pulse (starting from the top). A pulse on J1 that arrives with the reset counts as the new downbeat.

J1 and J2 read pulses and triggers, not held voltages: they're the audio inputs, and a steady voltage fades out of them within about a second. That's why J2's start/stop is a toggle rather than a held run gate.

Two CV inputs

In Settings, the top-right knob switches J7 and J8 between Outputs (the default) and CV in. As inputs they're CV A and CV B, ±5 V each, and outputs 5 and 6 go quiet.

Hold B2: the middle-right knob picks what CV A moves, the bottom-left knob what CV B moves. The choices are Off, Ratio, Wave, Width, Level, Offset, Phase, Steps, Fills, Rotate, Chance, Swing and Slew. Each one moves the output that was selected when you turned its knob, and the knob's ring takes that output's colour.

The CV adds to the setting rather than replacing it: +5 V is one full turn of the knob up, −5 V one full turn down. So −5 V into LEVEL silences an output, and +5 V into RATIO takes x1 all the way to x32. Your stored setting never changes; pull the cable and the output is as you left it.

Every output sits between 0 and +5 V at the default OFFSET of 0. Turn OFFSET down by half the LEVEL to centre an LFO on zero (outputs 5 and 6 start that way, at −2.5 V). Gates, triggers and envelopes rest at 0 V while the clock is stopped.

Recipes

Tresillo kick

  1. Select an output and set RATIO to x2, WAVE to Trig.
  2. Hold B3: STEPS to 8. It starts at 4 fills; turn FILLS to 3.
  3. ROTATE moves where the pattern starts in the bar.

Breathing filter

  1. WAVE to Tri, RATIO to /8 (two bars).
  2. WIDTH skews it: low for a slow rise and a quick fall.
  3. LEVEL and OFFSET fit it to the filter's range.

A looping random melody

  1. WAVE to Random, RATIO to x2, into a quantizer.
  2. Hold B3: a little SLEW for glide, CHANCE to taste.
  3. Hold B2: J2 to Reset, and send it a pulse every bar or two. The melody repeats.

Fills from a CV

  1. Settings: J7/J8 to CV in.
  2. Select your hi-hat output, hold B2, and turn the middle-right knob to Fills.
  3. An envelope or LFO into J7 now thickens and thins the pattern.

Setup, saving and flashing

Everything (the tempo, the J1 and J2 settings, the CV targets and all eight outputs) saves itself to preset slot 11 five seconds after you stop turning things, and comes back at the next power-up. The other firmwares in this family use other slots, so switching through the firmware picker keeps each one's state. Presets saved by hand from Settings hold the whole patch too.

A fresh module starts with: 1 quarter gates, 2 eighths, 3 sixteenth triggers, 4 a gate each bar, 5 a two-bar sine, 6 a four-bar triangle, 7 a 3-in-8 eighth-note pattern, and 8 a new random level each beat.

Build it from belt-alchemy/pulse with make libdaisy once in the repo root, then make, which gives build/pulse_alchemy.bin. Put it in /alchemy on the card and pick it from the firmware picker, or flash it like any Alchemy Lab firmware with the web programmer.
