The idea

What it does

One Lab runs a bass line, a lead line and four drum triggers. The two lines are generated, and they are made of chord tones, so when the progression moves they move with it. You shape them with a handful of knobs and keep the ones you like.

Knobs are thresholds

Turn it back and the line comes back

Every step owns a fixed random number. DENSITY is a threshold against it, so raising DENSITY only adds notes, and lowering it brings back exactly the line you had. LENGTH works the same way for note lengths.

Chords

Lines follow the progression

A step stores "the 2nd chord tone", not "E". A locked line still re-voices on every chord change. The default is A minor, i – VI – III – VII, a bar each.

Drums

Programmed, generated, or both

Steps you enter (on the Launchpad, or in a text file on the SD card) plus a generator on the pots: density thresholds or Euclidean rhythms, added on top. Works with no controller plugged in.

Keep, roll, undo

Eight slots per voice

ROLL makes a new line, MUTATE changes a quarter of it, UNDO swaps the last one back (again for redo), LOCK freezes it. Each voice has 8 pattern slots and the drums 16, queued to the next bar.

The panel

Four pages · click any control

B1 cycles the pots through the bass, the lead and the drums. Hold B3 for Setup. The voice pots edit the pattern that is playing now; when another pattern takes over, the pots have to catch its values before they move anything, so nothing jumps.

On the Launchpad
DRUMS mode: four drums, two rows each, 16 steps. Tap for on or off; SHIFT and tap for 75, 50, 25 %. Grey pads are generated; tapping one programs it.

    
On the pots, no controller needed
B1 to the DRUMS page. KICK, SNARE, HAT and PERC set how much the generator adds; STYLE picks Gen 1–4 or Euclid 1–4; PATTERN picks which of the 16 plays, at the next bar. Each pattern keeps its own.

    
From the Mac: /seq/drums.txt
Write patterns as text (the box above does it for you), put the file on the card, power-cycle. The module reads it at power-up when it has changed, writes only the patterns it names, and switches their generator off so the file is what plays. B2 flashes green when it took it, red when a line made no sense (nothing changes then). The file stays on the card.

    
    
Getting the file onto the card
Take the microSD card out of the Lab, make a folder called seq at the top, and save the text as drums.txt inside it. Put the card back and power the Lab up.

    
Drum presets
A preset already keeps the drums: Settings (B2+B3 for 2 s), pots 2 and 3, saves or loads slots 0–10, and so does the web programmer. A preset is the whole state, both voices included. To keep just drum ideas, use the 16 patterns, or keep several drums.txt files on the Mac.

    
Loop length per drum
In the file, a line's length is that drum's loop, 1 to 64 steps: a 16-step kick with a 12-step hat gives polymeter. The Launchpad edits the first 16 steps.

Clock and transport

  
Internal tempo until something clocks J1

  

    
The first pulse takes over
With nothing on J1 the Lab runs at TEMPO. The first rising edge on J1 switches to the external clock and plays step one on that edge. Between pulses the position is filled in from the measured period, and it never runs past where the next pulse will land.

    
Two seconds of silence stops it
If J1 goes quiet for 2 s the transport stops and control goes back to TEMPO. The next pulse starts it again from step one.

    
Stop means stop
B2, ▶ on the Launchpad, PLAY on the XL or Start on the Haute42 stops it even while J1 keeps clocking; the pulses keep being measured. Play again and it starts from step one on the next pulse, in time.

    
Reset is on the next pulse
A rising edge on J2 makes the next J1 pulse step one, the Eurorack convention. With no clock, reset rewinds at once. J1 and J2 are AC-coupled, so only edges count, never a held level.

    
Patterns change on the bar
A queued pattern, for a voice or the drums, takes over at the next bar line, so a change always lands on the one.

    
Resolution
96 ticks per quarter note inside. Clock pulses are read every millisecond, so an external pulse lands within 1 ms of where it arrived.

  






  
Setup, saving and flashing

  
What to know before the first power-up

  

    
It saves when you stop
Your patterns, knobs, locks, drums, tempo and key save themselves to preset slot 5 three seconds after the last change, but only while stopped. A save pauses the timing for a moment, which would drop beats mid-song. While playing with unsaved changes, B2 flashes magenta every two seconds. Powering off mid-jam loses what changed since the last stop.

    
Launchpad mode is the default
The front USB-C is either the host for the controllers (Launchpad) or the web programmer's port (Mac), one per boot. Hold B1 while powering up for Mac mode once. The choice lives in Settings (B2+B3 for 2 s), and every firmware on the card shares it.

    
Power for the controllers
The Lab's front port gives no 5 V. Use a powered USB hub or OTG adapter in front of it; the host handles one hub level with up to three devices, which is exactly Launchpad + XL + Haute42.

    
Other firmware on the same card
The SD picker in Settings swaps firmware. Each one keeps its own preset slot (seq 5, Meld 6, Warps 7, Clouds 8, Elements 9, Marbles 10, Plaits 11, Smack 12, Mark 13, Belt 14, Relay 15), so switching away and back loses nothing.
