Hypjolin puts Rob Hordijk's Benjolin, a stepped, rhythmic chaos box, next to Ian Fritz's Hypster, a smooth, swirling hyperchaos generator, and lets each one push the other around. It follows Triglav Modular's Hypjolin, a hand-built module they released for anyone to copy.

Two kinds of chaos

The Benjolin is a "noise box that is bent by design". Two oscillators feed a rungler, an eight-step shift register clocked by one oscillator and fed by the other. Its last three steps make a stepped voltage that goes back into the oscillators' pitch, so the whole thing keeps rearranging itself. The result sweeps a resonant filter. It is chaos in steps: rhythmic, gritty, sometimes almost a melody.

The Hypster is four integrators in a ring, with nonlinear stages between them. Turn it up and it stops repeating, in more than one direction at once. That is hyperchaos. Its four outputs, X, Y, Z and U, drift and swirl smoothly. Slow, they are a living modulation source. Fast, they are raw, dense audio.

On the original Hypjolin you patch the two together with banana cables. On the Alchemy Lab the patchbay is two pages: PATCH picks what feeds each input, DEPTH sets how much.

Benjolin · blue

Stepped chaos

Osc A and Osc B, 18 octaves each, from slow pulses to buzzing tones. The rungler pushes their pitch and the filter's cutoff. LOOP freezes the pattern into a repeating figure.

Outputs: triangle A, triangle B, pulse A, pulse B, XOR, PWM, rungler, lopass.

Hypster · purple

Smooth hyperchaos

RATE sets how fast the ring turns, from a slow drift to about 1.3 kHz. GAIN and DAMP push it between calm, periodic and wild. SHAPE and SYMM change how its orbit folds.

Outputs: X, Y, Z, U, and their inverses through a negative DEPTH.

The panel

Five pages of six knobs. B2 steps through the first four; hold B3 for DEPTH. Pick a page, then a knob.

B1The LOOP switch, on or off. Lit cyan when on, dim blue when off; its brightness follows the rungler. With B3 held: kick the Hypster back to life after it has stopped.
B2Next page: Jolin, Filter, Hyp, Patch. Its light is the page's colour (red if the processor is overloaded).
B3Hold for DEPTH. Its light shows the Hypster: X as red, Y as blue.

The Hypster, up close

The firmware's Hypster, running here, starting where the module starts: GAIN 1.76, DAMP 0.20, deep in the chaos. GAIN and DAMP are the two to learn first. Low GAIN with high DAMP and it winds down and stops. In between there are periodic islands, where it settles into a loop of two, three or four turns; around DAMP 0.25–0.3 is a good place to find one. SYMM off lets the cross-coupling through on one side only, which tends to give simpler, lopsided loops; SYMM on is where the densest chaos lives.

Press Listen to hear X and Y in stereo at RATE. It's raw on purpose, so start with the volume low.

The drawing runs the ring slower than RATE so you can follow it; Listen plays it at RATE. The ring is in volts, as the circuit's is: about ±10 V, with a limiter at ±9 V.

The rungler

Each time Osc B's pulse rises, the register shifts one step and a new bit comes in: Osc A's pulse, XOR the bit falling off the end. The last three bits, marked with a cyan line, are read as a number from 0 to 7. That is the rungler's stepped voltage, and RUN>A, RUN>B and RUN>F send it to the two oscillators and the filter.

With LOOP on, the LOOP knob biases Osc A's comparator. Near noon new bits still arrive. Towards either end the comparator stops changing and the register just goes round, so the pattern locks into a loop of 8 steps (or 16, when it comes back inverted).

Slowed down 40 times here so you can watch it step. On the module the same knobs reach audio rate.

Patching

The Hypjolin has six black input jacks, each with a cv attenuator: Osc A, Osc B and the filter on the Benjolin side, RATE, GAIN and DAMP on the Hypster side. On the Lab, PATCH (amber) gives each one a source, any of the twelve outputs or Off, and DEPTH (green, hold B3) sets how much. Noon is nothing, right is positive, left is inverted. That is how you get −X, −Y and the rest.

Full DEPTH with a full-scale source is 5 V at that input: five octaves on Osc A, Osc B, the filter and RATE, 1.5 on GAIN (whose range is 0.25–3), 1.0 on DAMP (range 0–2). The jacks J3–J6 add on top.

It boots already patched: X → Osc A at 0.3, Y → Filter at 0.25, Rungler → Rate at 0.3. So the Hypster wobbles the Benjolin's pitch and filter, and the Benjolin's steps kick the Hypster's speed about.

Closed loop

  1. PATCH: Rungler → Rate, X → Osc B.
  2. DEPTH: both about two o'clock.
  3. The Hypster's speed follows the rungler, and the rungler's clock follows the Hypster. Neither settles.

Hypster voice

  1. FILTER: OUT L = X, OUT R = Y.
  2. HYP: RATE past one o'clock (audio), GAIN up slowly from the bottom.
  3. A stereo pair that goes from a hum to a roar. Or set INPUT = X to send it through the filter.

Stepped melody

  1. JOLIN: OSC A at audio, OSC B slow (a few Hz), RUN>A up.
  2. B1 for LOOP, then the LOOP knob to one end.
  3. An 8- or 16-step riff. Turn it back towards noon to let it mutate.

Breathing drone

  1. HYP: RATE low (a slow drift), SYMM on.
  2. PATCH: Y → Filter, Z → Gain; DEPTH gentle.
  3. The filter breathes while the Hypster changes how wild it is by itself.

Setup, saving and flashing

Everything you set, every page plus the LOOP switch, saves itself to the module's preset slot 1 five seconds after you stop turning things, and comes back as you left it. The first boot after flashing starts from the defaults above, already moving.

Build it from hypjolin-alchemy with make libdaisy once, then make, which gives build/hypjolin_alchemy.bin. Put it on the card or flash it like any Alchemy Lab firmware.

Where it comes from

The Hypjolin is by Triglav Modular in Budapest: an edition of ten, commissioned by Goodiepal and Bananskolen, released anti-copyright with its circuit, panel files and manual at github.com/triglav-modular/Hypjolin. It combines the Benjolin, Rob Hordijk's design, and the Hypster, Ian Fritz's, whose 2018 write-up includes its schematic.

This firmware models those circuits from that material. It isn't affiliated with Triglav, Rob Hordijk's estate or Ian Fritz, and it hasn't been compared by ear with the real thing. Some details had to be invented to fit the Lab: the PATCH and DEPTH pages, how the LOOP knob locks the rungler, the filter's exact model, and the Hypster's overall loop gain. Fritz tuned his by simulation in a spreadsheet; this one was tuned by measuring how chaotic each setting of GAIN and DAMP is. The design notes mark every such choice.
