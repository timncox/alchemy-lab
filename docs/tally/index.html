The idea

What it does

Tally is one oscillator with a lot of ways to bend it. It starts as a pure sine on J9 and a cosine on J10, a stereo pair a quarter-cycle apart. Three controls reshape it, and a fourth, MORPH, changes what those three do. Everything else is there to move them: a second oscillator, a function generator, and a stepped pattern that can sequence it. It is written in the spirit of fancyyyyy × grrrwaaa's K-ACCUMULATOR, folded down from 32 HP onto the Lab's six knobs.

Centre

Always one move from a sine

With SHIFT, DEPTH, SHAPE and DAMPED all the way down (and STRETCH and the Mods at rest), the output is a clean sine and cosine wherever MORPH points. Learn the way back here and the rest makes sense: move one control, listen, return.

The morph ring

Eight ways to bend it

MORPH travels round eight waveshaping nodes: formant, feedback, two-operator and cross phase modulation, asymmetric. SHIFT, DEPTH and SHAPE keep similar jobs on each, and between nodes the sound blends continuously.

The UFG

Function generator, three jobs at once

A rise-and-fall generator from 0.02 Hz to audio rate. It modulates the oscillator, gates it (DAMPED: from soft animation to hard sync and enveloped hits) and clocks the pattern. Out on J7.

The pattern

A sequencer that wanders and comes back

A 2–16 step pattern drawn from the second oscillator. CHANCE lets new values in without ever overwriting the loop, so turning it back down always returns the original. Out on J8, and it can play the oscillator's pitch.

The panel

Five pages · click any control

B2 steps through four pages: OSC, UFG, Pattern and Mods. B2's light is the page's colour. Hold B3 for the fifth, Root, which holds the tuning and the Mod oscillator. Knobs catch: after a page change a knob does nothing until it passes the value it had, so nothing jumps.

The morph ring

Tally's oscillator, running in this page

Eight nodes sit round MORPH's ring. Their LEDs say how each one works: green nodes feed the oscillator back into itself, yellow ones are modulated by the Mod oscillator, red ones modulate each other both ways. Move the sliders: the scope draws the sine (J9) and cosine (J10), the figure on the right plots one against the other, and the spectrum shows which harmonics are there. UFG and Damped

The function generator

One rise, one fall

TIME sets the rate: 0.02 Hz to about 4 kHz, or relative to Root when ROUTE sends Root to the UFG. SKEW tilts it from a falling ramp through a triangle to a rising ramp, and sets the length of its gate. U.SHAPE bends the curve: linear, sine, exponential, raised cosine.

Loop or one-shot

LOOP runs it continuously. ONE-SHOT waits for a trigger from B1 or the jack mapped to UFG trigger (J5 by default), runs one cycle and stops.

Four trigger types

Sync restarts it, but lets the current cycle finish first, so triggers faster than the UFG lock it to a subharmonic. Reverse flips its direction. The two Sustain types do the same and also hold at the peak while the gate stays high.

DAMPED / PULSAR

Syncs the oscillator to the UFG and gates it by the UFG at once. Low: gentle animation. Middle: the oscillator pulls into step. Full: hard sync and full gating. With a slow one-shot UFG that is an enveloped hit; at audio rate it is pulsar synthesis.

STRETCH

Lives on the UFG page. It moves the harmonics that SHIFT and SHAPE create up and away from the harmonic series while the fundamental stays put. With no waveshaping it does nothing at all.

Out on J7

The UFG's level, 0–5 V, updated every half millisecond. B1's light follows it too.

The pattern

Delta-sigma steps, clocked by the UFG

Where the values come from

Each time the UFG comes round, the pattern advances a step. New values are taken from the Mod oscillator and squeezed into eight levels, so the Mod's pitch against the UFG's rate decides how busy or smooth they are.

CHANCE never overwrites

At zero the pattern is a locked loop. Turning CHANCE up lets new values play in place of stored ones, but nothing stored changes, so turning it back down always returns the original loop.

LENGTH zooms

2 to 16 steps, with 8 in a dead zone at noon. Shortening loops the stretch that starts where you made the change, without touching the rest.

SMOOTH

Glides between steps. Which steps glide is part of the pattern, so the same loop glides the same way every time. Past noon every step slews; at audio rate it acts as a filter.

Editing with B1

On the Pattern page: tap B1 to write a fresh value at the current step, double-tap to undo it, hold for a second to write a whole new pattern. The pattern is saved, so it survives a power cycle.

Where it goes

J8 outputs it as 0–5 V. PITCH sends it to the oscillator's pitch, up to ±2 octaves, quantised when a scale is on. On the Mods page, SOURCE can make it the modulation instead of the UFG.

Root, scales and the Mod

Hold B3

ROOT is Tally's tuning reference: C4 at noon, ±3 octaves, plus 1 V/oct from J3. ROUTE sends it to the oscillator, the UFG, both, or neither. With B3 held, tap B1 to choose the scale system: off (B3 grey), 12-TET (blue) or just intonation (amber). SCALE then picks the scale, and the pitch snaps to it.

The Mod oscillator

A second sine that phase-modulates the first on the yellow and red nodes. HARMONIC sets its pitch relative to what it follows (Root by default; Settings can make it the oscillator or the UFG). Above noon it climbs the harmonics, below noon it drops freely, up to four octaves.

ORDER

How HARMONIC behaves: Free (a smooth ±4-octave offset), Even, All or Odd (blending through that set of harmonics), or Quantised (snapped to the scale, or to simple ratios with no scale).

DETUNE

Pulls the Mod off pitch, up to a semitone and fine near noon. It also drifts the wavefolder's harmonics, so with SHAPE up it sets the folds moving.

Q.Trig

In Settings. Each time the quantised pitch moves to a new note, the UFG is triggered. With the UFG on one-shot and an LFO into J3, Tally arpeggiates itself.

The CV map

J4, J5 and J6 go wherever you send them

J3 is always V/Oct. The other three CV inputs can each drive any knob on any page, a Settings choice, or a button action, with an amount. Out of the box they are MORPH, UFG trigger and SHAPE.

  1. Hold B2 + B3 for two seconds to open Settings, then tap B1 to step through its pages to CV to.
  2. Each row of knobs is one jack: top J4, middle J5, bottom J6. The left knob picks the section: Off, OSC, UFG, Pattern, Mods, Root, Settings or Actions. The right knob picks the knob in that section by its place on the panel, P1 (top left) to P6 (bottom right). So UFG + P4 is STRETCH and OSC + P2 is FREQ.
  3. Tap B1 once more for CV amount: the left knob of each row is that jack's amount. Full right is +1, noon is off, full left inverts.
  4. Press B2 or B3 to leave. The map saves with everything else.

How far it goes

On a knob, ±5 V sweeps its whole range. FREQ, ROOT and TIME follow 1 V/oct. MORPH moves 1.6 nodes per volt and wraps round the ring.

Selectors and Settings

0–5 V steps through every option, starting from where the knob is set.

Actions

Gates: on above 1.5 V, off below 0.5 V. Each fires once per rising edge, except UFG trigger, which holds while the gate is high (the Sustain trigger types use that).

What doesn't move

CV moves the sound, not the knob rings, and a CV-driven Settings choice never changes the saved setting.

Setup, saving and flashing

What to know before the first power-up

First boot is Centre

After a flash Tally starts at Centre: a C4 sine and cosine, every waveshaper and attenuverter at rest, a slow looping UFG, a locked 8-step pattern.

It saves itself

All five pages, the settings, the scale system and the pattern save to preset slot 2 five seconds after the last change, with your hands off the buttons.

Settings

B2 + B3 for two seconds; B1 steps pages; B2 or B3 leaves. Page 1: brightness, presets, Ext (J1) (Off, Sync, Track) and Mod tracks (Root, OSC, UFG). Page 2: the SD firmware picker and Q.Trig. Pages 3 and 4: the CV map.

EXT on J1

Sync: J1's rising zero crossings replace the UFG as the damped-sync leader. Track: the oscillator follows J1's pitch (pitch only, not level), and FREQ becomes a ratio to it. Feed it a square wave and get a sine and cosine back, with all the waveshaping on top.

Other firmware on the same card

The SD picker swaps firmware. Each one keeps its own preset slot (Tally 2, Stencil 3, Break 4, Seq 5, Meld 6, Warps 7, Clouds 8, Elements 9, Marbles 10, Plaits 11, Smack 12, Mark 13, Belt 14, Relay 15), so switching away and back loses nothing.

Lights

B1 follows the UFG (on the Pattern page, the pattern's value). B2 is the page colour, red if the processor runs hot. B3 is the scale system. At boot the P1 ring shows the last session's worst processor load for 2.5 seconds.

Getting it

Tally isn't released yet. It has been built and tested on a computer, and its first run on a module is next. When it's out, it installs like every other instrument on the Alchemy Lab page: flash the first one with Hermetic's web programmer, then carry it on the card and switch to it from the picker.

Coming from a K-Accumulator

Same ideas, a smaller panel, our own maths

Tally isn't a port. The K-Accumulator's firmware isn't public, and Tally contains none of it. Tally was written from the K-Accumulator quick-start guide's descriptions. Where the guide only names an algorithm (damped sync, harmonic stretching, the delta-sigma encoding, the scale tables), the maths here is our own and will sound different.

Status

Firmware 0.1.0 · not yet released

Checked

  • 339 automatic tests on a computer: Centre is a pure sine at every morph position, pitch and both tuning systems, every node and control changing the sound, a 40,000-block sweep of every control staying in range, STRETCH keeping the fundamental, the UFG, damped gating, the pattern's lock, length and restore, pitch tracking, and the CV map's scaling.
  • The whole firmware runs, unchanged, in an Alchemy Lab emulator: Centre and V/Oct, the waveshapers, page cycling, the UFG on J7, struck hits from J5 and B1, the pattern on J8, the scale system, and jacks remapped over USB and then played.

Not yet tried

  • Anything on a real module: the processor load, the output level, the J7 and J8 outputs, the Settings pages under real fingers.
  • The sound itself. Every range was tuned by tests, not yet by ear.
