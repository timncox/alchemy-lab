How to play it

  • Knobs: drag up or down. The mouse wheel works too, and a double-click puts a knob back in the middle.
  • Buttons: click and hold B1, B2 and B3, or click the panel and use the keys 1, 2 and 3. Holding two at once (for Settings and other combos) is easiest with the keys.
  • CV jacks: the sliders under the panel are what you'd patch into J3 to J8. Drag for a voltage, or click GATE for 0 or +5 V.
  • What each control does: every instrument's manual explains it: .
  • Switching instruments restarts the page with the new one. Settings and saved presets last until you leave the page.

Test your own firmware

Writing firmware for the Alchemy Lab? The emulator runs yours too, on your own computer: any firmware built on the Alchemy SDK, such as Hermetic's template, compiled against its own copy of the SDK. You get the same panel, plus test scripts that turn knobs, press buttons, send CV and check the sound and the lights. An AI coding assistant can do the setup and write the tests. Open your firmware's folder in Claude Code, Codex or Cursor and paste this:

Clone https://github.com/timncox/alchemy-lab (branch emu-custom), read emu/AGENTS.md, and get this firmware running in the Alchemy Lab emulator. Then write emulator tests for its main controls and run them.

It needs a Mac or Linux (on Windows, WSL) with SDL2 installed; no ARM toolchain. The guide the assistant follows is AGENTS.md, and you can follow it by hand too. The emulator can't tell you about CPU load: a patch that runs here can still be too heavy for the module.

What's real and what isn't

Real: every line of each instrument's firmware, the Alchemy SDK's own framework (pages, Settings, LED rings, presets) and the FAT file system on the SD card. Imitated: the panel, the codec, the 64 MB SD card, the flash chip, and the USB controllers. Not imitated: the module's processor speed, so a sound that works here can still be too heavy for the real thing; and the bootloader, the card picker and HostLink.
